## Why this step matters

The ERP answers "what happens after the stimulus?". The power spectrum (<En>PSD</En>) answers a different question: which frequencies are in the signal, and how much <En>power</En> does each one have?

The spectrum is a core analysis tool, in resting-state and sleep studies for example. It is also the fastest check of your data quality: a clear alpha peak means a healthy brain signal, and a narrow peak at 50 or 60 Hz means line noise.

We compute it here on the continuous data after removing ICA components and before the 30 Hz <En>low-pass</En>, so the full spectrum up to 64 Hz is visible.

## Steps in the GUI

Start with the dataset `D1 main clean` open, as in "Starting point". This is a continuous dataset, not epochs. The starting-point script reruns ICA, so it may take a few minutes, unless you use the shortcut line at its top.

1. Open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel spectra and maps</MenuPath>.
2. The <UiLabel>Channel spectra and maps -- pop_spectopo()</UiLabel> window opens. Keep <UiLabel>Epoch time range to analyze [min_ms max_ms]:</UiLabel> at its value, that is, the whole recording.
3. Keep <UiLabel>Percent data to sample (1 to 100):</UiLabel> at `100`.
4. In <UiLabel>Frequencies to plot as scalp maps (Hz):</UiLabel>, type `10` instead of the default `6 10 22`.
5. In <UiLabel>Plotting frequency range [lo_Hz hi_Hz]:</UiLabel>, type `1 64` instead of the default `2 25`. The default hides line noise and everything above 25 Hz.
6. Keep <UiLabel>Spectral and scalp map options (see topoplot):</UiLabel> as it is, and press <UiLabel>Ok</UiLabel>.
7. Read the figure in this order:
   - **Alpha peak:** a clear rise near 10 Hz. The scalp map above it shows where it is strongest.
   - **1/f decline:** power falls gradually as frequency rises. This is the normal shape of every EEG signal.
   - **Narrow peaks or dips:** a narrow peak at 60 Hz in uncleaned data means line noise. In our data you will find the opposite: a narrow dip between 58 and 62 Hz, left by the line-noise filter.
8. Click any line to see its channel name.

## The equivalent script

```matlab
% Spectra of all channels from 1 to 64 Hz, with a scalp map at 10 Hz
figure;
[spectra, freqs] = pop_spectopo(EEG, 1, [0 EEG.xmax*1000], 'EEG', ...
    'freq', 10, 'freqrange', [1 64], 'electrodes', 'off');

% spectra: channels x frequencies, in 10*log10(uV^2/Hz)
fprintf('Frequency step: %.2f Hz\n', freqs(2) - freqs(1));

% Alpha peak at Pz between 8 and 13 Hz
pz = find(strcmp({EEG.chanlocs.labels}, 'Pz'));
alphaBand = freqs >= 8 & freqs <= 13;
alphaFreqs = freqs(alphaBand);
[peakPow, iPeak] = max(spectra(pz, alphaBand));
fprintf('Alpha peak at Pz: %.0f Hz, %.1f dB\n', alphaFreqs(iPeak), peakPow);

% Channels with the strongest 10 Hz power
[~, order] = sort(spectra(:, freqs == 10), 'descend');
disp({EEG.chanlocs(order(1:5)).labels});
```

## Which value should I choose?

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Plotting range | 1 to 64 Hz here (up to half the sampling rate) | The default 2 to 25 Hz hides line noise and muscle activity. To check quality, plot the full range. |
| Scalp map frequencies | 10 Hz for alpha | You can add others, such as 6 for theta and 20 for beta, depending on your question. |
| Percent of data | 100 | Lower values are faster, but on continuous data they use only the first part of the recording. D1 is small, so there is no need. |
| Window length | The default (1 second, 128 points) | Gives a frequency resolution of 1 Hz, enough to see the alpha peak. |
| Which data | Continuous, clean, before the low-pass | The 30 Hz low-pass removes everything above 30 Hz, so you would not see the full spectrum. |

## Common mistakes

- **Judging the data from the default plotting range.** From 2 to 25 Hz you cannot see 50 or 60 Hz at all. Change the range.
- **Reading the decline as a problem.** Power naturally falls with frequency (the 1/f decline). The problem is an unexpected narrow peak, or one channel whose spectrum is far from the others.
- **Forgetting the vertical axis is logarithmic.** A 10 dB difference means 10 times more power, not 10 microvolts.
- **Computing the spectrum on very short epochs.** Epochs shorter than the computation window lower the resolution. For an overall spectrum, continuous data are better.
- **Assuming the line frequency.** It is 60 Hz in Saudi Arabia and the US, and 50 Hz in Europe and most other Arab countries. Look at the spectrum before you assume.

## How to check it worked

- `freqs(2) - freqs(1)` equals 1 Hz, and `size(spectra)` gives 32 channels by the number of frequencies.
- The alpha peak at Pz is at roughly 9 to 11 Hz.
- The 10 Hz map is strongest at posterior channels, such as <En>POz</En> and <En>Pz</En>.
- At 58 to 62 Hz there is a narrow dip, not a peak.
