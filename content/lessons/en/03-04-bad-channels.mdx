## Why this step matters

A bad channel is a channel that does not record brain activity properly. The cause is often an electrode with poor skin contact, a damaged wire, or two electrodes bridged by gel.

It shows up in three ways: a flat line, much more noise than its neighbors, or a signal unlike the channels next to it.

One bad channel harms all the data. In an average reference its noise enters every channel. In ICA it takes components for itself instead of the real sources.

So you find bad channels now, record their names, and remove them. In the next lesson you rebuild them by interpolation.

<Callout type="note">
**About D1:** the official tutorial says the tutorial data are relatively clean and do not contain channels you would normally reject. And the clean_rawdata check with the settings in this lesson does not remove any channel. This is a valid result: not all data have bad channels. We will not invent a fault. You learn the method on D1 and see what a bad channel looks like in a picture from other data.
</Callout>

## Steps in the GUI

Start with the main dataset `D1 hp0.1 notch60` loaded, as in the Starting point.

**First: look at the signal**

1. Open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel data (scroll)</MenuPath>.
2. Scroll through the whole recording, not just the first few seconds. Some channels go bad for a short time and then recover.
3. Look for three signs: a flat line, an amplitude much larger than the neighboring channels, or repeated jumps in a single channel.
4. In D1 you will see large peaks in `EOG1`, `EOG2`, and the frontal channels. These are eye blinks, a normal artifact that ICA removes later. A channel is not bad because it records blinks.

**Second: look at the spectrum**

1. Open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel spectra and maps</MenuPath>.
2. Change <UiLabel>Plotting frequency range [lo_Hz hi_Hz]:</UiLabel> to `1 64`, then press <UiLabel>OK</UiLabel>.
3. A bad channel shows up as a line far from the group: much higher, or much lower if it is flat.
4. Click any line in the plot, and its channel number appears in the command window.
5. In D1 the lines stay close together, and no line clearly stands apart.
6. To look closely at one suspect channel, open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel properties</MenuPath> and type its number. The window shows the channel's location on the head, its activity over time, and its spectrum together.

**Third: check with the clean_rawdata channel criteria only**

1. Open <MenuPath>Tools > Reject data using Clean Rawdata and ASR</MenuPath>.
2. Leave <UiLabel>Remove channel drift (data not already high-pass filtered)</UiLabel> unchecked, because the data are already filtered.
3. Keep <UiLabel>Process/remove channels</UiLabel> checked.
4. Check <UiLabel>Ignore these channels (ECG, EMG, ...)</UiLabel> and type `EOG1 EOG2` in the field next to it.
5. Leave the three criteria at their defaults: <UiLabel>Remove channel if it is flat for more than (seconds)</UiLabel> at 5, <UiLabel>Max acceptable high-frequency noise std dev</UiLabel> at 4, and <UiLabel>Min acceptable correlation with nearby chans [0-1]</UiLabel> at 0.8.
6. Uncheck <UiLabel>Perform Artifact Subspace Reconstruction bad burst correction/rejection</UiLabel>, and uncheck <UiLabel>Additional removal of bad data periods</UiLabel>. These two remove time segments, which is the topic of another lesson.
7. Press <UiLabel>OK</UiLabel>. The command window prints `Scanning for bad channels...` and then its progress over 5-second blocks.
8. A window comparing the data before and after appears. A removed channel shows in red. In D1 no channel is red.
9. Name the resulting dataset `D1 badch` and leave <UiLabel>Overwrite it in memory (set=yes; unset=create a new dataset)</UiLabel> unchecked.

**Fourth: record the names and remove what you found by eye**

1. Write down the names of all the bad channels you found, from all three methods. You will need them for interpolation and for the Methods section of your paper.
2. clean_rawdata removes what it detects by itself. A channel you found only by eye, remove it with <MenuPath>Edit > Select data</MenuPath>: type its name in <UiLabel>Channel(s)</UiLabel>, check the box under <UiLabel>on->remove these</UiLabel> on the same row, then press <UiLabel>OK</UiLabel>.
3. In D1 the list is empty, so you remove nothing. Save `D1 badch` with <MenuPath>File > Save current dataset as</MenuPath>. The interpolation lesson starts from it.

## The equivalent script

```matlab
% Detection with clean_rawdata channel criteria only:
% no drift removal, no ASR, no window rejection, EOG channels ignored
EEG_test = pop_clean_rawdata(EEG, 'FlatlineCriterion', 5, 'ChannelCriterion', 0.8, ...
    'LineNoiseCriterion', 4, 'Highpass', 'off', 'BurstCriterion', 'off', ...
    'WindowCriterion', 'off', 'BurstRejection', 'off', 'Distance', 'Euclidian', ...
    'channels_ignore', {'EOG1', 'EOG2'});

% Names of the channels it removed
badChans = setdiff({EEG.chanlocs.labels}, {EEG_test.chanlocs.labels}, 'stable');

% Add channels you found by eye, e.g. badChans = [badChans {'T7'}];
fprintf('Bad channels: %s\n', strjoin(badChans, ', '));

% Remove them by name from the main dataset
if ~isempty(badChans)
    EEG = pop_select(EEG, 'rmchannel', badChans);
end
EEG.setname = 'D1 badch';
EEG = pop_saveset(EEG, 'filename', 'D1_badch.set', 'filepath', pwd);
```

On D1 the script prints `Bad channels: ` with an empty list, and the channel count stays 32. Copy `badChans` into the Starting point script of the following lessons, which start with the same variable.

## Which value should I choose?

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Flat line | 5 seconds | The clean_rawdata default. A channel that stays flat longer than that records nothing. |
| High-frequency noise | 4 standard deviations | The default. It compares each channel with the other channels, so it catches a channel that is clearly noisier. |
| Correlation with nearby channels | 0.8 | The default, also given in the official tutorial. A channel is removed if its correlation stays below 0.8 for more than half of the recording. It depends on electrode locations, so make sure they are correct. |
| Eye and heart channels | Excluded from detection | clean_rawdata is designed for EEG channels. An eye channel is naturally unlike its neighbors, so it may be removed although it is fine. |
| ASR and segment removal | Off in this lesson | Our goal here is channels only. Segment removal is in the clean_rawdata & ASR lesson. |

<Callout type="note">
**An automated alternative:** the PREP extension (PrepPipeline) detects bad channels and computes an average reference that resists bad channels, all automatically. Install it from <MenuPath>File > Manage EEGLAB extensions</MenuPath>. We start here with the manual method so you understand each step.
</Callout>

## Common mistakes

- **Not excluding the eye channels.** Without excluding `EOG1` and `EOG2`, clean_rawdata may remove `EOG1` in D1, because its signal is unlike the neighboring channels. It is not bad: it is a non-brain channel by nature. The clean_rawdata documentation itself says that channels carrying non-brain signals are usually removed this way. That is why the official clean_rawdata page may show red channels on the same data.
- **Treating a blink channel as bad.** Large peaks in the frontal channels are eye blinks. ICA removes them later, and the channel is not removed because of them.
- **Looking only at the first few seconds.** Some channels go bad in the middle of a recording. Scroll through all of it.
- **Removing a channel without recording its name.** You need the names for interpolation, for the Methods section, and to process the other participants the same way.
- **Removing many channels without asking why.** If many channels look bad, first check the electrode locations and the whole recording. clean_rawdata itself stops with an error if it would remove more than 75% of the channels, and says the cause is usually wrong electrode locations.

## How to check it worked

1. **Channel count:** in the main window, <UiLabel>Channels per frame</UiLabel> equals the original number minus the removed channels. In D1 it stays 32.
2. **Your list is written down:** you have the names of the removed channels, or "none" as in D1.
3. **Spectrum after removal:** open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel spectra and maps</MenuPath> with the range `1 64`. No line clearly stands apart from the rest.
4. **The eye channels are still there:** `EOG1` and `EOG2` are still in the channel list.
