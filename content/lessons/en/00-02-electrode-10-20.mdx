## The idea

Every channel in your data has a name such as Fz, C3 or PO7. The name is an address: it tells you where the electrode sat on the head. The channel is the signal recorded from that electrode.

You will often read sentences like "blinks are strong at Fp1" or "the P3 component is largest at Pz". So how do you find an electrode from its name alone?

## How do I read an electrode name?

<Figure src="electrode-10-20/electrode-10-20.svg" alt="The 10-20 system. Left: side view with the arc from nasion to inion split into 10%, four times 20%, then 10%, carrying Fpz, Fz, Cz, Pz and Oz. Right: top view with the 21 electrodes; odd numbers on the left of the head, even on the right, z on the midline." source="original" />

Every name has two parts: **letters for the region, then a number or z for the side.**

| Letter | Region | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Fp | Front of the forehead, above the eyes (frontopolar) | Fp1, Fp2 |
| F | Frontal lobe | F3, Fz |
| C | Central area around the central sulcus, not a separate lobe | C3, Cz |
| T | Temporal lobe | T7, T8 |
| P | Parietal lobe | P3, Pz |
| O | Occipital lobe at the back of the head | O1, O2 |

- **Odd number = left**, **even number = right**. Left means the participant's left, not yours as you face them.
- **z = the midline** from nose to the back of the head (from "zero"). It is a lowercase z, not the number 2: Cz is exactly in the middle, and C2 is just right of it.
- **The larger the number, the farther from the midline:** C5 is farther out than C3, and C3 farther than C1.
- **Two letters = an electrode between two regions:** FC5 between F and C, PO7 between P and O.

Example: P4 sits over the parietal lobe (P), in the right half (even number).

## Why is it called 10-20?

Because electrodes are not placed in millimeters but as proportions of each participant's head. The line is measured from the nasion (top of the nose) to the inion (the bump at the back of the head). Fpz sits at 10% of its length, then Fz, Cz, Pz and Oz each 20% further, leaving 10% to the inion. The same applies along the line between the ears: T7, then C3, then Cz, then C4, then T8.

So an electrode's actual location differs a little between heads and caps, but its name stays the same.

The original system has only 21 electrodes. Modern systems add electrodes between these positions:

| System | Step between electrodes | Where you see it |
|---|---|---|
| 10-20 | 10% or 20%, 21 electrodes | Routine clinical recording |
| 10-10 | 10%, with extra electrodes halfway along each distance | `eeglab_data.set` (32 channels), and the PhysioNet file in station 2 (64 channels) |
| 10-5 | 5%, for high-density systems (Oostenveld and Praamstra 2001) | Systems with 128 channels or more |

## An old paper says T3 or T5. Where are they?

The 10-10 system changed four old names because they did not follow the rule (ACNS guideline, 2016):

| Old name | Modern name |
|---|---|
| T3 and T4 | T7 and T8 |
| T5 and T6 | P7 and P8 |

The old names still appear in many books, papers and devices. If you read T5, it means P7.

## Why do some channels not follow these names?

Because they are not on the scalp at all. In `eeglab_data.set` the channels `EOG1` and `EOG2` record eye movement. In other data you will find M1 and M2 on the mastoid bones behind the ears, or A1 and A2 on the earlobes, often used as the reference.

## Where do I see my data's electrodes in EEGLAB?

When you have a dataset with electrode locations, open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel locations > By name</MenuPath>. You see the head from above with the nose up, and each electrode name in its place. The left of the plot is the participant's left. You will learn to add locations from <MenuPath>Edit > Channel locations</MenuPath> in the [channel locations lesson](/en/lesson/channel-locations/).

To see the electrodes of the dataset you will use through most of the path right now, run this script after the [installation lesson](/en/lesson/install-eeglab/). It reads only the location file and does not open the dataset itself.

```matlab
% Plot the 32-channel montage of the EEGLAB sample dataset with labels
eeglab nogui;                                         % load EEGLAB functions only
eeglabRoot = fileparts(which('eeglab'));
locs = readlocs(fullfile(eeglabRoot, 'sample_data', 'eeglab_chan32.locs'));

figure;
topoplot([], locs, 'style', 'blank', 'electrodes', 'labelpoint');
title('eeglab\_data.set montage');

disp({locs.labels});                                  % list all channel names
```

You will see 32 labeled dots, with Cz exactly in the center. Thirty are 10-10 names and two are not: `EOG1` and `EOG2`, the two dots farthest outside the head circle. Edge channels such as `T7`, `O1` and `Oz` sit just outside the circle, because they are below the head's mid-plane (EEGLAB tutorial).

## Test yourself: can you read the names?

Answer before you read the answer, without looking at the table.

- **Where is FC5?** Answer: between the frontal lobe and the central area, in the left half, fairly far from the midline.
- **Which is closer to the midline: F3 or F7?** Answer: F3, because its number is smaller.
- **Is C2 the same as Cz?** Answer: no. Cz is exactly in the middle, and C2 is just right of it.
- **An old paper reports a peak at T6. What is its modern name?** Answer: P8.
