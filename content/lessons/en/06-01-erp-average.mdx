## The idea

In each epoch, the brain's response to the square is a few microvolts, buried under larger unrelated activity. You can hardly see it in one trial.

But the response repeats with the same shape and timing in every trial, while the other activity varies at random. Average the epochs point by point: the response stays; the random activity cancels out.

The result is the event-related potential (<En>ERP</En>), the basis for measurement, condition comparisons, and statistics. How do you compute it in EEGLAB, and how do you read it?

## What happens to the waveform as trials add up?

One trial looks like noise. With 10 trials the waveform starts to appear, and with all 77, a clear positive peak appears after 400 ms.

## How do I see each channel's average from the menus?

EEGLAB has no separate "compute average" menu. The average is computed inside the ERP plotting menus, or with one line in a script. Here we display it once for a first look; station 7 covers the plotting options in detail.

Start with the dataset `D1 epochs clean` open. If you don't have it, open "Script to reach this state" at the top of the lesson.

1. Open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel ERPs > In scalp/rect. array</MenuPath>.
2. The <UiLabel>Topographic ERP plot - pop_plottopo()</UiLabel> window opens. Keep <UiLabel>Channels to plot</UiLabel> at `1:32`, leave <UiLabel>Plot single trials</UiLabel> and <UiLabel>Plot in rect. array</UiLabel> unchecked, and press <UiLabel>Ok</UiLabel>.
3. The average of each channel appears at its place on the head. Click the <En>Pz</En> trace to enlarge it in its own window.
4. In the enlarged trace, look for two things after the vertical line at zero:
   - A small negative wave around 200 ms, clearest at posterior channels such as <En>POz</En> and <En>Oz</En>.
   - A large positive wave peaking around 400 ms, largest at central and parietal channels such as <En>Cz</En> and <En>Pz</En>.

<Callout type="note" title="Axis direction">
In this window, positive is up (the `'ydir', 1` option in the last box). Some books and programs plot negative up, so always check the axis labels before you say "positive peak".
</Callout>

## How do I compute the average and find the peak in a script?

```matlab
% The ERP is the mean over epochs: channels x time points
% (the same line pop_plottopo computes in the GUI step)
erp = mean(EEG.data, 3);
fprintf('Averaged %d epochs\n', EEG.trials);

% Plot the ERP at Pz, positive up
pz = find(strcmp({EEG.chanlocs.labels}, 'Pz'));
figure;
plot(EEG.times, erp(pz, :), 'k', 'LineWidth', 1.5);
xlim([-200 800]); grid on;
xline(0, '--'); yline(0);
xlabel('Time (ms)'); ylabel('Amplitude (\muV)');
title(sprintf('ERP at Pz, %d epochs', EEG.trials));

% Approximate latency of the largest positive peak between 250 and 600 ms
win = EEG.times >= 250 & EEG.times <= 600;
winTimes = EEG.times(win);
[peakAmp, iPeak] = max(erp(pz, win));
fprintf('Positive peak at Pz: %.1f uV at %.0f ms\n', peakAmp, winTimes(iPeak));

% Quick look at all channels, same as the GUI step
figure; pop_plottopo(EEG, 1:EEG.nbchan, EEG.setname, 0, 'ydir', 1);
```

## What goes into the average, and how many trials do I need?

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| What goes into the average | Clean epochs from one condition only | The average mixes whatever you give it. With two conditions, compute one average per condition. |
| Number of trials | As many as you can keep after rejection, and about the same number per condition | Noise in the average falls with the square root of the number of trials: 4 times the trials means half the noise. |
| Peak search window | Set before you look at the data, from the literature | The 250 to 600 ms window here is for one example only. Precise amplitude and latency measurement comes in the "ERPLAB: measuring amplitude & latency" lesson. |

## How do I check that the average is right?

- `size(erp)` returns `32 384`: channels × time points, with no epoch dimension.
- At Pz there is a positive peak between roughly 300 and 500 ms, of about 10 µV. The official tutorial notes that the largest ERP variance in these data is at about 430 ms.
- The waveform before zero (the baseline period) is close to zero and fairly flat.
- If you plot the average of only 10 epochs, the waveform is rougher than the average of all epochs.

## Which mistakes spoil the average?

- **Mixing conditions in one average.** In D1 the square appeared at two positions (the `position` field). Averaging both positions together is fine as an example here, but comparing positions needs one average per position. Comparing conditions is covered in the channel ERP plotting lesson in station 7.
- **Reading the highest peak as "the component".** A peak at one channel can come from more than one overlapping ERP component. The approximate latency here is for recognizing the waveform, not for a scientific conclusion.
- **Confusing the axis direction.** A positive wave plotted downward is misread as negative. Check the axis.
- **Averaging before rejection.** One epoch with a large artifact is enough to change the average. Reject first, as in [the epoch rejection lesson](/en/lesson/epoch-rejection/).
- **Very different trial counts between conditions.** A condition with 20 trials and another with 80 differ in noise, so their peaks look different even when the response is the same.
