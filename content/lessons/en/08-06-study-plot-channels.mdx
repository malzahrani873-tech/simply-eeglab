## Why this step matters

In the channel ERP lesson (station 7) you read one participant's curve: time on the horizontal axis, amplitude in µV on the vertical one, and two conditions in two colors. **The same reading applies here.** The difference is that each curve is now the average of 14 participants (or 7 per group), not the average of one person's trials.

The average hides differences between people. One participant with a very large amplitude can create a "difference" in the average that the others do not have. So this lesson teaches two things together: plotting the average, and looking at each participant before you trust the average.

## Steps in the GUI

**About the data (D6):** `animal_mine.study` with its three designs from the design lesson, and the measures from the last lesson: ERPs, spectra and ERSPs at all channels (ERSPs on a light grid: 20 frequencies from 3 to 30 Hz).

**1. Open the plot window**

1. Run the "Starting point" script. It computes the measures only if it cannot find them.
2. Open <MenuPath>Study > Plot channel measures</MenuPath>. The <UiLabel>View and edit current channels -- pop_chanplot()</UiLabel> window opens.
3. At the top is <UiLabel>Select design:</UiLabel>. Choose `image x gender`.
4. The left column, <UiLabel>Select channel to plot</UiLabel>, holds the channel list and the buttons that plot the average: <UiLabel>Plot ERPs</UiLabel>, <UiLabel>Plot spectra</UiLabel> and <UiLabel>Plot ERSPs</UiLabel>. Next to each button is <UiLabel>Params</UiLabel> for its options.
5. The right column, <UiLabel>Select subject(s) to plot</UiLabel>, holds a list starting with <UiLabel>All subjects</UiLabel> followed by the participant codes, and the buttons <UiLabel>Plot ERP(s)</UiLabel>, <UiLabel>Plot spectra</UiLabel> and <UiLabel>Plot ERSP(s)</UiLabel> that plot participants.

**2. The group ERP at Pz**

1. Choose `Pz` in the left list.
2. Press <UiLabel>Params</UiLabel> next to <UiLabel>Plot ERPs</UiLabel>. The <UiLabel>ERP plotting options -- pop_erpparams()</UiLabel> window opens.
3. Tick <UiLabel>Plot first variable on the same panel</UiLabel>, so animal and distractor appear in one panel. Leave <UiLabel>Plot second variable on the same panel</UiLabel> unticked, so each group has its own panel. Leave <UiLabel>Time range (ms) [low high]</UiLabel> empty for the whole epoch. Press <UiLabel>Ok</UiLabel>.
4. Press <UiLabel>Plot ERPs</UiLabel>. Two panels appear, F and M, each with the curves of both pictures.
5. Read them as in the station 7 lesson: where do the two curves start to separate? Is the difference similar in both panels? Look first at the y axis and its direction.

**3. Each participant**

1. With `Pz` selected, choose <UiLabel>All subjects</UiLabel> in the right list, then press <UiLabel>Plot ERP(s)</UiLabel>. A thin curve appears for each participant.
2. Look for a curve far from the others. Choose its code in the list and press <UiLabel>Plot ERP(s)</UiLabel> to see that participant alone.

**4. A scalp map over a time window**

1. Change <UiLabel>Select design:</UiLabel> to `image only`, so all participants are plotted together.
2. Press <UiLabel>Sel. all</UiLabel> to select every channel.
3. Press <UiLabel>Params</UiLabel> next to <UiLabel>Plot ERPs</UiLabel>. First type `150 250` in <UiLabel>Time range (ms) [low high]</UiLabel>, then choose <UiLabel>Plot averaged topography over time range</UiLabel> in <UiLabel>Multiple channels selection</UiLabel>. The order matters: if you choose the map while the time is empty, the list returns to the first option and a warning appears: `Select time range first for plotting topography, then select that setting again.` Press <UiLabel>Ok</UiLabel>.
4. Press <UiLabel>Plot ERPs</UiLabel>. Two maps appear: the 150 to 250 ms mean for each picture.
5. Afterwards set <UiLabel>Multiple channels selection</UiLabel> back to <UiLabel>Plot channels individually</UiLabel> and clear <UiLabel>Time range (ms) [low high]</UiLabel>. Going back alone does not clear the time, so every later ERP plot stays cut to 150 to 250 ms.

The 150 to 250 ms window here is an example for learning. In your research the window is chosen before looking at the difference, from earlier studies or from your question, not from the plot itself. The reason is in the next lesson, on statistics.

**5. Spectrum and ERSP**

1. Select `Oz` alone. Press <UiLabel>Params</UiLabel> next to <UiLabel>Plot spectra</UiLabel>, type `3 40` in <UiLabel>Frequency [low_Hz high_Hz]</UiLabel>, and tick <UiLabel>Plot first variable on the same panel</UiLabel>. Then <UiLabel>Plot spectra</UiLabel>.
2. Select `Pz` alone, then <UiLabel>Plot ERSPs</UiLabel>. A time × frequency image appears for each picture.
3. ERSPs were computed at every channel, so you can select any channel. The plot covers 3 to 30 Hz only, because that is the grid computed in the last lesson.

## The matching script

```matlab
% STUDY and ALLEEG come from the starting point script (design 2 is current)
names = {STUDY.changrp.name};
pz = names(strcmpi(names, 'Pz'));
oz = names(strcmpi(names, 'Oz'));

% Group ERP at Pz: both pictures on one panel, one panel per gender (design 2)
STUDY = pop_erpparams(STUDY, 'plotconditions', 'together', 'plotgroups', 'apart', 'topotime', []);
STUDY = std_erpplot(STUDY, ALLEEG, 'channels', pz, 'design', 2);

% The same channel for every participant
STUDY = std_erpplot(STUDY, ALLEEG, 'channels', pz, 'plotsubjects', 'on', 'design', 2);

% Scalp maps of the mean ERP from 150 to 250 ms, all participants together (design 3)
STUDY = pop_erpparams(STUDY, 'topotime', [150 250]);
STUDY = std_erpplot(STUDY, ALLEEG, 'channels', names, 'design', 3);
STUDY = pop_erpparams(STUDY, 'topotime', []);

% Group spectrum at Oz and ERSP at Pz (design 3)
STUDY = pop_specparams(STUDY, 'plotconditions', 'together', 'freqrange', [3 40]);
STUDY = std_specplot(STUDY, ALLEEG, 'channels', oz, 'design', 3);
STUDY = std_erspplot(STUDY, ALLEEG, 'channels', pz, 'design', 3);
```

## Which value should I choose?

| Option | In this lesson | When to change it |
|---|---|---|
| Design | 2 to compare the pictures within each gender, and 3 for the maps, spectra and ERSPs | Always plot the design that will go into the statistics. |
| Conditions on one panel | Yes (`plotconditions` = `together`) | Separate panels when you want to see the shape of each curve alone. |
| Groups on one panel | No (`plotgroups` = `apart`) | One panel gets crowded with four curves. |
| Time range | Empty, meaning the whole epoch | Narrow it for publication only, after you have seen everything. |
| y limits (<UiLabel>Plot limits [low high]</UiLabel>) | Empty | Fix them when you compare different plots, so a change of scale does not fool you. |
| <UiLabel>Lowpass plotted data [Hz]</UiLabel> | Empty | It changes the plot only, not the data. Do not use it to make a weak curve look better. |
| Map window | 150 to 250 ms (example) | From your question or earlier studies, before looking at the result. |

## Common mistakes

- **The wrong design in <UiLabel>Select design:</UiLabel>.** The window remembers the last choice. Look at the number of panels: design 2 gives two panels, and design 3 one panel.
- **Trusting the average without looking at the participants.** If one or two participants create the difference, the average does not represent the group.
- **Choosing the map window after seeing the difference.** This greatly raises the chance of finding a "difference" even in random data. We explain this in the statistics lesson.
- **ERSPs computed at a few channels only.** In EEGLAB 2026.1, reading them back fails with `Unrecognized field name "chan1"`. Recompute at every channel, as the start block does.
- **Comparing two panels on different scales.** EEGLAB picks the scale for each plot. Fix <UiLabel>Plot limits [low high]</UiLabel> before comparing.
