## The idea

An ERP waveform on screen looks simple, but a reader needs three things to understand it: which direction is positive, what unit each axis has, and how many trials went into the average.

A figure without these three is easy to misread. A peak "going up" may be negative in a flipped plot, and a difference between two conditions may come from different trial counts rather than from the brain.

So how do you plot channel ERPs in EEGLAB, and compare two conditions in a figure nobody misreads?

## How do I plot all channel ERPs from the menus?

Start with the dataset `D1 epochs clean` open. If you don't have it, open "Script to reach this state" at the top of the lesson.

**1. All channel ERPs at their scalp positions**

1. Open <MenuPath>Plot > Channel ERPs > In scalp/rect. array</MenuPath>. The window <UiLabel>Topographic ERP plot - pop_plottopo()</UiLabel> appears.
2. Leave <UiLabel>Channels to plot</UiLabel> at `1:32`.
3. Leave <UiLabel>Plot single trials</UiLabel> and <UiLabel>Plot in rect. array</UiLabel> unchecked.
4. Note that <UiLabel>Other plot options (see help)</UiLabel> contains `'ydir', 1`. This means positive up. Leave it and press <UiLabel>Ok</UiLabel>.
5. One waveform appears for each channel at its approximate position on the head, nose up.
6. Click the `Pz` waveform. It opens in its own window with full axes. This is where you read amplitude and latency.

**2. The same plot in columns**

Open the same window again and check <UiLabel>Plot in rect. array</UiLabel>. The channels appear in an ordered grid. The grid is clearer for reading numbers; the scalp layout is clearer for seeing the distribution.

## How do I compare two conditions on one axis?

In D1, each `square` event has a `position` field with value 1 or 2: where the square appeared on the screen. These are our two conditions. Both positions are in the left visual field, at about 5.5 and 2.7 degrees from the center, so expect a small difference between them and no difference between the two halves of the head.

According to the official tutorial, EEGLAB menus compare conditions through a STUDY, even with a single participant, and the STUDY is the topic of station 8. Here we compare with a short script, because it shows what happens inside any comparison plot: split the trials, average each part, and plot both on one axis.

<Callout type="warning">
Do not use <MenuPath>Edit > Select epochs or events</MenuPath> to split D1 in EEGLAB 2026.1. In this release, the tool stops on epoched data with "All epochs have been removed, empty dataset", even when many epochs remain. The script below splits by epoch indices directly and avoids the problem.
</Callout>

**The difference wave**

The difference wave is one condition minus the other, at every time point. It shows when and where the two conditions differ, in one curve instead of two.

- The last part of the script below stores the two conditions as datasets in EEGLAB, prints their numbers (for example `Datasets for Sum/Compare ERPs: 2 (position 1) and 3 (position 2)`), and calls `pop_comperp` once.
- A plot appears with three curves at each channel: position 1, position 2, and their difference (position 1 minus position 2), positive up because of `'tplotopt', {'ydir', 1}`. Click `Pz` to open it on its own.

Optional: the same plot has a menu, <MenuPath>Plot > Sum/Compare ERPs</MenuPath>, but it is hidden by default in EEGLAB 2026.1. It appears only after you check <UiLabel>If set, show all menu items from previous EEGLAB versions. You must restart EEGLAB for this to take effect.</UiLabel> in <MenuPath>File > Preferences</MenuPath> and restart EEGLAB. This option changes how every menu looks compared with the images in these lessons, so turn it off again afterward.

## How do I split the trials and plot both conditions in a script?

Copy the full block into the command window.

```matlab
EEG = pop_loadset('D1_epochs_clean.set', pwd);

% All channels at their scalp positions, positive up
figure; pop_plottopo(EEG, 1:EEG.nbchan, 'D1 epochs clean', 0, 'ydir', 1);

% Stimulus position of the time-locking 'square' event in each epoch
pos = eeg_getepochevent(EEG, 'type', {'square'}, 'timewin', [-5 5], 'fieldname', 'position');
fprintf('Position 1: %d epochs, position 2: %d epochs\n', sum(pos == 1), sum(pos == 2));

% One dataset per condition, for later use
EEG_pos1 = pop_select(EEG, 'trial', find(pos == 1));
EEG_pos2 = pop_select(EEG, 'trial', find(pos == 2));
EEG_pos1.setname = 'D1 epochs clean pos1';
EEG_pos2.setname = 'D1 epochs clean pos2';

% Both conditions on one axis at Pz
chan = find(strcmp({EEG.chanlocs.labels}, 'Pz'));
erp1 = mean(EEG_pos1.data(chan, :, :), 3);
erp2 = mean(EEG_pos2.data(chan, :, :), 3);

figure; hold on;
plot(EEG.times, erp1, 'LineWidth', 1.5);
plot(EEG.times, erp2, 'LineWidth', 1.5);
xline(0, 'k:');
yline(0, 'k-');
xlim([-200 800]);
set(gca, 'YDir', 'normal');   % positive up
xlabel('Time (ms)');
ylabel('Potential (\muV)');
legend({sprintf('Position 1 (n = %d)', EEG_pos1.trials), ...
        sprintf('Position 2 (n = %d)', EEG_pos2.trials)}, 'Location', 'best');
title('Pz');

% Both conditions as datasets in EEGLAB, for Plot > Sum/Compare ERPs
[ALLEEG, ~, idx1] = eeg_store(ALLEEG, EEG_pos1);
[ALLEEG, ~, idx2] = eeg_store(ALLEEG, EEG_pos2);
eeglab redraw;
fprintf('Datasets for Sum/Compare ERPs: %d (position 1) and %d (position 2)\n', idx1, idx2);

% Difference wave at every channel: position 1, position 2, and position 1 minus position 2
pop_comperp(ALLEEG, 1, idx1, idx2, 'addavg', 'on', 'subavg', 'on', 'diffavg', 'on', 'diffstd', 'off', 'tplotopt', {'ydir', 1});
```

## Which polarity, range, and channel should I choose?

| Setting | Value | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Polarity | Positive up (`'ydir', 1`) | The default in EEGLAB 2026.1 menus (`icadefs.m`: `YDIR = 1`). Some fields have traditionally plotted negative up. Whatever you choose, state it on the axis or in the caption. |
| Plot range | `-200 800` ms | Shows the whole baseline and the post-stimulus period until the response ends. The epoch is longer (-1 to 2 s), but the ends add nothing to this figure. |
| Channel | `Pz` in this example | A midline posterior channel where the response to this visual task is clear. In your research, choose the channel from previous studies before you see your result. |
| Vertical limits | The same limits for every plot you compare | Different automatic limits make a small difference look large. Use `ylim` with the same values. |
| Trial count | Always in the legend or caption | An average of 20 trials is noisier than an average of 40. Readers need the number to judge. |

## How do I check that the figure is right?

- **The counts add up:** the trial counts of the two conditions sum to `EEG.trials`, and each is close to 40 (40 and 40 before rejection).
- **The polarity is right:** the positive peak in the Pz plot points up, as in the menu window after clicking Pz.
- **The two plots agree:** the overall average at Pz in `pop_plottopo` lies between the two condition curves of the script plot.
- **The figure is complete:** both axis labels with units, the time-zero line, and a legend with trial counts.
- **The difference wave** at Pz averages 0 µV from -200 to 0 ms, because baseline correction set that mean to zero in both conditions. Its single points still swing above and below zero, and over the whole epoch these swings are about as large as the difference after 0 ms. The reason: with about 38 trials per condition in one recording, noise alone produces differences of this size. That is why a conclusion needs a group of participants (station 8).

<Methods>
ERPs were averaged separately for each stimulus position and plotted at Pz from -200 to 800 ms with positive voltage up (pop_plottopo, EEGLAB 2026.1).
</Methods>

## Which mistakes cause a figure to be misread?

- **A script that flips the axis without you knowing.** Called without `'ydir'`, `plottopo` plots negative up, while the menu plots positive up. Write `'ydir', 1` explicitly in every script.
- **Axes without units.** "Time (ms)" and "Potential (µV)" are not decoration. Without them the reader cannot tell whether the axis is in seconds or samples.
- **Comparing conditions with very different trial counts.** Noise in an average falls as trials increase, so a peak in the condition with fewer trials may look larger by chance. Report the counts, and be careful when they differ a lot.
- **Concluding a difference from one participant.** The difference between positions 1 and 2 here describes this recording only. A conclusion needs a group of participants and statistics (station 8).
- **Plotting an ERP from continuous data.** Plotting needs epochs. If the <MenuPath>Plot > Channel ERPs</MenuPath> menu is grayed out, the open dataset is not epoched.
